Most nostr clients today are firehoses. Notes fly past, some brilliant, most noise. We rely on manually-curated follows, mute lists, or relays with simplistic algos to filter signal. What if, instead, users had their own living, evolving Knowledge Graph curated with input from their personal web of trust? 

Enter Brainstorm: a smart nostr relay that runs the GrapeRank algorithm to manage your Grapevine. And your Grapevine is your personal assistent to help you determine who is the most trustworthy to curate content, facts, and information in any given context.

At first glance it sounds like yet another feed algorithm. It isn’t. Brainstorm is the first serious attempt since the 1980s–2000s to solve the original problem of Good Old-Fashioned AI: how to build a large, queryable, reasoned-over symbolic knowledge base that actually grows in the real world. The most intensive effort was lead by Lenat, who spent 40 years and hundreds of millions of dollars trying to hand-craft Cyc into “the one true ontology.” The result? After encoding roughly 25 million axioms, Cyc still could not reliably answer everyday questions that a bright ten-year-old (or a $20 LLM fine-tuned for two days) gets right. Why? The answer is widely known: the knowledge-acquisition bottleneck. It failed because building an intelligent system required human experts to manually translate all necessary real-world knowledge into explicit, formal symbols and logical rules — a process that was slow, astronomically expensive, brittle, and fundamentally unscalable.

But where GOFAI failed, Brainstorm will succeed. With Brainstorm, knowledge acquistion does not depend on a centralized, failed process.

## The AI Connection

It just so happens that academics have been trying -- and failing -- for decades to achieve almost exactly the same goal as Brainstorm. Called symbolic AI, aka Good Old Fashioned AI (GOFAI). The core idea of GOFAI is that intelligence = the ability to manipulate explicit rules and symbols. Where did those rules and symbols come from? They had to be hand-crafted by humans. GOFAI’s holy grail (especially in the Cyc era) was a giant, hand-curated (or semi-automatically curated), logically consistent knowledge base that contains commonsense and domain knowledge in machine-readable form.

Brainstorm’s personalized Knowledge Graph is exactly that: except the curation is distributed and trust-weighted rather than done by a central team of knowledge engineers.

GOFAI, by the way, is a wholely different beast than connectionist AI: neural networks, statistical learning, machine learning, etc. The LLMs that have attracted billions since 2017 has stolen attention from GOFAI, and not everyone is happy about that. Many academics, like Josh Tenenbaum, view GOFAI as the superior alternative to connectionist AI, the billions of dollars of investment a tragic misallocation of resources.
